Great question. Let me work through the structure first, then the strategy.

   ---

   ### The board

   Top (5 slots) ── 1000 pts ── 200 pts/slot
   Bottom Right (5 slots) ── 550 pts ── 110 pts/slot ── GATES Bottom Left
   Bottom Left (5 slots) ── 550 pts ── 110 pts/slot ── only reachable after BR

   Key insight: BR isn't worth 550 — it's worth 1,100, because if you hold BR, BL is never even touched. A BR hold
   denies 2× the points of a top hold.

   ---

   ### Three strategies

   A) The gatekeeper wall (my recommendation)

   Top:  6,  7,  8,  9, 10
   BR:   1,  2,  3,  4,  5   ← wall
   BL:  11, 12, 13, 14, 15

   Put your 5 best in BR. The opponent faces an absolute wall before they can touch BL. If they can't crack it, they
   lose 1,100 points — more than top is worth. Top is defended by teams 6-10, still very strong. Even if they clear top
    (1,000 pts), they're stuck.

   The opponent's dilemma: they can throw their best attackers at top for 1,000 pts, but then their remaining attackers
    can't beat your wall. Or they can throw everything at the wall — and then top holds too.

   B) Balanced split

   Top:  1, 3, 5, 7, 9
   BR:   2, 4, 6, 8, 10
   BL:  11, 12, 13, 14, 15

   Both premium zones get top-tier teams interleaved. The opponent can't focus-fire one zone — every zone has a mix of
   must-burn and manageable. If they overcommit to top, BR holds. If they overcommit to BR, top holds. This is harder
   for the opponent to solve than A, but gives up the pure wall.

   C) Top-heavy (common but wrong)

   Top:  1,  2,  3,  4,  5
   BR:   6,  7,  8,  9, 10
   BL:  11, 12, 13, 14, 15

   Over-defends the 1,000 pt zone but leaves BR beatable. Opponent clears top with their best, then walks through BR
   (teams 6-10) with their second-best, then BL with their third-best. They get 2,100. This is what most players do
   instinctively — and it's wrong.

   ---

   ### Why A wins

   The gating structure means BR is the leverage point. A single hold in BR denies 1,100 points. A single hold in top
   denies only 1,000. And a BR hold also denies BL, so the opponent's leftover attackers have nothing to swing at.

   The opponent's counterplay to A is to ignore BR entirely and just clear top — but then they cap at 1,000 points out
   of 2,100 possible, and you've defended the bottom half of the board with zero effort spent.

   The only case where C is better is if the opponent is so outmatched that they can clear everything regardless of
   placement — but then placement doesn't matter anyway.
